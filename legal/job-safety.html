Job safety: how to spot a job scam

Last updated October 23, 2026.

UAply shows jobs from employers' own public hiring systems and career pages, from USAJOBS, and from well-known internship lists. Each card says where the job came from. We choose the sources we use, but we cannot check every posting, so a fake job can still get through. Here is how to spot one, and what to do.

Warning signs

  • They ask you to pay. For training, equipment, a background check, a "starter kit" or a visa fee. A real employer does not charge you to get a job.
  • They send you a check. They ask you to deposit it and send part of the money back, or buy gift cards or equipment with it. The check bounces later and the loss is yours.
  • They want bank details or your Social Security number before any offer. These belong in the hiring paperwork after a real offer, not in a first message.
  • The interview happens only by text or a chat app, or you get an offer without any interview at all.
  • The pay is far above what the work is worth, for very little experience or a few hours a week.
  • The email comes from a personal address (a free email service) instead of the company's own domain, or the company name is slightly misspelled.
  • They rush you. "Reply today or lose the job." Pressure is how scammers stop you from checking.

What to do

  • Stop. Do not send money, gift cards or bank details, and do not deposit a check from someone you have not met through a real hiring process.
  • Check with the company directly. Find its careers page or phone number yourself (not from the message) and ask whether the job and the person are real.
  • Report the job on UAply. On the job card, tap the flag button (Report this job) at the top right of the card, next to Share, pick what is wrong and send it. The UAply team reads every report, and the job is skipped for you.
  • Report the scam to the authorities. In the US: the Federal Trade Commission at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and online fraud to the FBI at ic3.gov.
  • If you already paid or shared details, contact your bank or card company right away, and see IdentityTheft.gov if your Social Security number was shared.

What UAply will never ask for

UAply never asks you to pay an employer, and never asks for your bank account, your Social Security number, a password or a verification code by email, text or chat. If a message claiming to be from UAply asks for any of these, it is not from us: tell us at uaplytechnologies@uaply.net.
