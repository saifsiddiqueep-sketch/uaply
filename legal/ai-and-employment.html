AI and matching

Last updated October 16, 2026.

Draft, not reviewed by a lawyer. Laws on automated tools in hiring are changing quickly. Have an employment attorney check the notices below, and keep this page true if the product changes.

The short version

  • The match percentage on each card is a fixed formula, not artificial intelligence or machine learning.
  • It only helps order jobs for you. It never rejects you, hides you from an employer, or decides anything about hiring.
  • UAply does not use generative AI on your profile or applications. Our help assistant can use generative AI to answer support questions, but it only sees what you type into the chat, is clearly labeled, and takes no part in matching or applying (see "The help assistant" below).
  • Employers make every hiring decision.

How the match score works

For each listing, UAply compares what the listing asks for with what you entered, and adds points:

PartPointsHow it is judged
Skills30The share of the listing's skills that are in your profile, or that your headline, About you or major says in so many words (the card shows how many came from there)
Experience15Your years of experience against the years the listing asks for
Job title15Whether the listing's job title matches one of the up to five target titles you entered (common equivalents such as "developer" and "engineer" count). Full points if you entered no titles
Pay15Whether the listed pay reaches your minimum (partial credit if pay is not listed)
Work style10Remote, hybrid or on-site against what you accept (full points if you tick none)
Location10Your city against the listing's location, or willingness to relocate. In some versions of UAply it also uses how far the listing is from you, or an estimated travel time if you choose public transit, bike or walking (see below)
Education5Your highest education against what the listing asks for

The score is also capped at 45% when one of these is a clear miss: the listed pay is well below your minimum, the work style is one you did not tick, or you have less than half the years of experience the listing asks for. The card then says so. The cap never hides a listing from you.

In some versions of UAply, two more parts can appear. Their points come out of the parts above, so the total is still 100:

PartPointsHow it is judged
Field10Appears only if you picked fields you want to work in. Full points if the listing's field, read from its job title, is one you picked, and none if not. 5 points come out of Skills and 5 out of Job title
License10Appears only if the listing requires a license or certification (health care, dental and legal listings only). Full points if you added what it requires. Holding any one of the licenses the job is for counts, so a job open to "NP or PA" needs only one of them. Missing an extra such as BLS or CPR, ACLS or notary lowers the points, and missing the license the job is for gives none. 5 points come out of Skills and 5 out of Experience

In those versions, a listing is capped at a 40% match when you have added none of the licenses the job is for, such as an RN license or bar admission, and the License part says so. BLS or CPR, ACLS and notary are extras: missing one lowers the License part but never caps or hides a listing. The License part only compares the licenses you tick with what a listing says it requires, and UAply does not check that you hold them. The score itself never hides a listing. The Filters option "Hide jobs that need a license I have not added" does, it is on by default, and you can switch it off at any time. "Hide fields I did not pick" works the same way and hides nothing until you pick fields.

UAply reads a listing's field from its job title. For health care, dental and legal listings only, it also reads which licenses the listing requires. A licensed job title counts, such as "Registered Nurse" or "Associate Counsel". In the text of the listing, only wording about holding a license counts, and never when the words next to it say "preferred", "a plus" or something similar, or when it sits under a "Preferred qualifications" heading. A licensed title also raises the education the listing is read as asking for, for example a doctorate for titles in law, medicine, dentistry, pharmacy and psychology, a master's degree for nurse practitioners, physician assistants and licensed therapists, and an associate degree for registered nurses and dental hygienists. Job titles also match common equivalents in these fields, such as nurse and RN, or attorney, lawyer and counsel. These are fixed rules applied to a listing's words, so they can miss or misread a listing. The listing's own page is the final word on what it requires.

Travel times are estimates, not a trip planner. In some versions of UAply you can say you get to work by public transit, bike or on foot, and the Location part then compares an estimated trip time with the limit you set (60 minutes if you set none). A trip within your limit scores full points, a trip up to one and a half times the limit scores half points, and a longer one scores none, or partial points if you said you would relocate. The estimate uses the straight-line distance plus an allowance for streets, at typical door-to-door speeds, and it only knows the city a listing is in, so it can be well off for a particular office. Check the route before you apply.

Each card shows the points for every part, so you can see exactly why a score is what it is. You can ignore the score and swipe on any listing. You can also set your own minimum match, or leave it at zero to see everything.

What the score does not use

The score never uses your age, race, ethnicity, sex, gender, religion, national origin, disability, veteran status or similar characteristics. UAply offers optional voluntary questions about some of these only so you can copy your answers into employer forms; those answers stay on your device and are never part of the score, filters or ordering. Your optional work authorization answers and your GPA are not part of the score either. The filters you choose in Filters (hiding roles that do not match your target titles, work styles you did not pick, jobs that pay below your minimum, distance, and so on) hide listings from your own list only, and each can be switched off at any time. Your opportunity types only decide which section opens first. We know that inputs like location and education can sometimes track protected characteristics indirectly, which is why the score never removes a listing on its own and why we show the full breakdown.

The help assistant

The Help button opens UAply Assistant, an automated assistant, never a person. It says so at the start of every conversation and shows a label: AI when its answers are written by generative AI, or Automated when they come straight from UAply's help articles. It answers only from those articles, names the article it used, and can be wrong.

  • It sees only what you type into the chat. It cannot see your profile, applications or account, and it cannot apply, change or delete anything for you.
  • When AI answers are on, your messages are sent to our AI provider to write the reply. UAply does not keep chat messages; it keeps only the feedback you choose to send.
  • It does not give legal, immigration or financial advice, and it does not take part in matching, ordering or applying.
  • You can reach a person from the chat at any time with "Talk to a person".

The optional "Message the hiring manager" feature fills in a fixed template from your profile and the listing. It does not use generative AI.

Notice for job candidates

When you apply through UAply, the employer receives the details you chose to give. UAply does not screen, rank or reject applicants on an employer's behalf. If an employer uses its own automated tool to evaluate you, that employer, not UAply, is responsible for telling you and for following the law that applies to it. Examples of those laws, current as of this page's date:

  • New York City (Local Law 144): employers using automated employment decision tools must have a recent independent bias audit, post a summary, and notify candidates.
  • Illinois (amended Human Rights Act, in effect January 1, 2026): employers must not use AI that discriminates and must notify people when AI is used in employment decisions.
  • California: Civil Rights Council rules (in effect October 1, 2025) cover automated decision systems in employment, and the California Consumer Privacy Act's rules on automated decision-making technology have compliance dates beginning in 2026 and 2027.
  • Colorado: a replacement automated decision-making law takes effect January 1, 2027.

Rules differ by place and change often. Ask an employer directly if you want to know whether it uses automated tools on applications.

For employers

If you receive applications through UAply, you stay responsible for your own hiring decisions and for any legal notices, audits and accommodations that apply to the tools you use. UAply's match score is a sorting aid for candidates, not an assessment of them, and you should not treat it as one. Licenses and certifications that come with an application are as stated by the applicant. UAply does not verify them, so confirm any license you rely on yourself.

Changes

If UAply begins using machine learning, generative AI or any automated step that affects what an employer sees about you, we will update this page and tell you in the app before it applies to you.
